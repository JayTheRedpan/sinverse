## Overview

Released in **Year 71**, at the height of the [Giantess Renaissance's](era-the-giantess-renaissance) consumer boom, **The Domesticator** is a SinCorp-catalogue **posture exoskeleton** — a powered frame fitted over a [toy](toys) that places him on all fours and keeps him there. The marketing copy calls it *"the pet he was always meant to be"*; the product manual, with SinCorp's usual clinical cheer, calls it a **behavioural enclosure system**.

The device serves three markets at once, and SinCorp is candid about all of them. For the giantess who wants the **experience of keeping a pet** — something small, loyal, and at her heel — it delivers exactly that. For enthusiasts of formal **pet play**, it provides the posture, the discipline, and the accessories as a complete boxed set. And for the owner of a **persistently disobedient toy**, it is a correctional appliance of last resort: a toy in a Domesticator does not misbehave, because a toy in a Domesticator does not *do* anything at all. The frame does it for him.

## Design and operation

The Domesticator is a rigid, articulated exoskeleton — braces along the limbs and spine, a harness across the torso, servos at every joint — that enforces a **quadrupedal stance** as its single, unvarying premise. Once sealed in, the toy stands, walks, sits, stays, and presents on command. The unit's design leaves the **stomach exposed**: partly a practical choice, and partly, as the catalogue notes with uncharacteristic poetry, *"an honest posture — the belly shown, the oldest animal gesture of trust there is."*

The defining feature is where the control sits. **The occupant has none.** The frame answers exclusively to its registered handler; a giantess issues commands — spoken, gestured, or sent from her band — and the exoskeleton executes them with the toy, in the words of one satisfied review, simply *"along for the ride."* He is a passenger in his own body, carried through heel and stay and beg by machinery that never asks his opinion. SinCorp's engineers regard this as the product's central elegance: obedience is no longer a behaviour to be trained, but a **specification to be installed**.

Every unit ships with a **colour-coded ID band**, letting a household, a district, or a kennel-club register its animals at a glance.

## The models

The line ships in two configurations, and their catalogue names — chosen with SinCorp's characteristic bluntness — tell a buyer everything about the intended role.

### The Bitch Model

The standard configuration, tuned for **containment and keeping**. The frame is compact, the power supply is rated for exceptionally **long unattended operation**, and the unit ships with a bundled **augmentation kit** — a suite of conditioning treatments that settle the occupant into placid, resilient docility, with a durability-and-elasticity package drawn from the standard [modification catalogue](toy-modifications) so that he holds up to the life the frame commits him to. An optional **sensory-deprivation mask** completes the fitting for owners who prefer their pet's world reduced to touch, command, and nothing else.

### The Alpha Model

The larger, heavier flagship, built around a **higher-output power pack** and marketed on a single word: *productive*. The Alpha's bundled **enhancement kit** runs opposite to the Bitch Model's — where one settles its occupant down, the other keeps him in a state of **permanent, chemically maintained readiness and drive**, dosed around the clock and never permitted to tire. Alpha units are the fixture of estate breeding programmes and the [population projects](reproduction) of the Renaissance, where a single well-kept Alpha is valued as infrastructure rather than companionship. Owners describe them, without irony, as the household's prize stud.

The name carries duties beyond the breeding ledger. An Alpha is expected to *live up to its title*: within a multi-frame household it stands at the top of the kennel's pecking order, and its handler will routinely set it over the lesser frames — putting the household's Bitch Models **at the Alpha's disposal** as a matter of ordinary routine. The exercise has no practical purpose whatsoever; a framed toy cannot resist anything, least of all another frame. That is precisely the point. It is **hierarchy as theatre** — a standing reminder, delivered by one toy to another at a giantess's word, of exactly where each of them stands.

Nor does the Alpha's jurisdiction stop at the kennel door. Households keep it as one keeps a **guard dog**: the free-walking [toys](toys) of the estate — the staff, the favourites, the ones trusted to move about unframed — live and work under the Alpha's watch, and an owner who catches a whiff of sulking or slackness need only give the word. The Alpha is *set on* the offender, and the discipline it delivers is very much in keeping with the rest of the catalogue — memorable, humiliating, and administered by something the toy can neither outrun nor outrank. Renaissance households prized the arrangement for its tidy economy: the mistress never needs to raise a hand, or even her voice. Her displeasure arrives on four legs. For this reason (and for the obvious economics of the thing) **most households keep exactly one Alpha**; a second would spoil both the symbolism and the peace.

Historians of the period note something else in the arrangement. Three decades before [Project Sylph](project-sylph) existed, the Alpha Model was already sketching its silhouette: **a toy raised above other toys**, granted standing, licence, and a title within the order that keeps him — and, just as tellingly, *used the same way*. The Alpha keeping a household's toys in line at its mistress's word is precisely the office a Sylph would one day hold, minus the polish. The comparison flatters the Domesticator considerably — a Sylph's elevation is earned against criteria and carries genuine prestige, while an Alpha's is nothing but his owner's purchase order and the frame he was sealed into — but the lineage is hard to miss, and more than one scholar has called the Alpha Model *"the Sylph's crude ancestor: the first proof that the new world had room for a toy above the rest, provided the height was on loan."*

## Life inside the frame

For the toy, the Domesticator is less an outfit than a verdict. Days are structured entirely by the handler's command vocabulary — heel, stay, present, kennel — and the frame's tireless servos make each one as involuntary as a heartbeat. Long-term occupants describe a curious drift: with every movement decided elsewhere, attention narrows to the things the frame does not govern — warmth, scent, the sound of her voice, the colour of the band on the frame's collar. Owners call the result *settling in*. For the occupants of a household's lesser frames, the order has one more face: the Alpha, whose standing over them is exercised regularly and by design — not because anything requires it, but because being reminded is part of the fitting. The [Resistance](the-resistance) calls it something considerably less fond, and lists the device among the technologies it most despises.

Among giantesses the device's reputation is warmly domestic. A Domesticator at heel is a common sight in [Matriarch](matriarchs) households and Renaissance-era parks, and an entire accessory economy — leads, kennels, show-ribbons, competition circuits — grew around the line within a few years of launch.

## Did You Know?

- The Domesticator launched in **Year 71**, and its first production run reportedly sold out within a month.
- The catalogue names — the **Bitch Model** and the **Alpha Model** — are SinCorp's own, and the company has never once apologised for them.
- The frame answers **only to its registered handler**; the occupant's preferences are, per the manual, *"not a supported input."*
- The **exposed stomach** is a deliberate design signature — the belly shown, the oldest animal gesture of submission.
- **Colour-coded ID bands** let entire districts register their kept pets at a glance, and kennel-club show circuits judge on band presentation alongside gait and obedience.
- The Alpha Model is marketed as *infrastructure* — the catalogue files it under household productivity equipment.
- **Most households keep exactly one Alpha** — its standing over the lesser frames is the point, and a second would spoil the arrangement.
- The Alpha doubles as the household's **guard dog**: the estate's ordinary, unframed toys live under its watch, and an owner's displeasure is delivered by setting it on them — she never needs to raise her voice.
- Scholars have called the Alpha Model **"the Sylph's crude ancestor"** — a toy raised above other toys, thirty years before [Project Sylph](project-sylph) made elevation something a toy could actually earn.
